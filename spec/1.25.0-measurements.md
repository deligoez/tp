# tp 1.25.0 — measurements

## Rolling implement slots

This section measures `spec/backlog/rolling-implement.md` §4. The expected numbers below were
written and committed before any code of the change.

### Fixture

- **The repository.** One spec and six open tasks, `t1` to `t6`, with no dependencies and a
  `touches` each (`t<i>.txt`), so no two overlap. `commit_strategy` is `builtin`.
- **The gate.** `quality_gate` is `sleep 1` with a log line before and after: real duration, almost
  no CPU. No `worktree_setup_cmd` is set, since the gate needs no ignored file.
- **The units.** A scripted unit, spawned through `TP_RUNNER_SEAM`, sleeps for the agent's time,
  commits `t<i>.txt` and closes through the real `tp done <id> --commit <sha>`. The sleeps are
  uneven: 4 s for `t1` and `t4`, 1 s for the other four.
- **The logs.** The unit and the gate append their start and end to a file outside the
  repository, so the log never dirties the tree a gate passed on.
- **Each run** starts from a fresh copy of the template with `run_max_units` 6, at caps 1, 2 and 3,
  once with the branch's binary and once with the barrier binary built from `3158f7d8`.

### What each number means

- **Wall time:** the wall clock around `tp run`.
- **Idle slot-seconds:** `cap × wall time` minus the summed `duration_seconds` of every unit
  attempt in the run state. Cap 1 has one slot, so its figure is the driver's own overhead.
- **Peak gates:** the largest number of gate runs, unit and main-branch alike, that the gate log
  shows running at one instant.
- **Gate reuse:** of the closes on the main branch, how many reused the unit's gate (the
  `gate reused from the unit's worktree` notice in the integration logs). A close reuses only when
  nothing landed on the main branch between its worktree's add and its pick. At cap 1 the unit
  gates in the main tree and there is nothing to reuse, so the rate is n/a.
- **Solo reopens:** tasks marked `solo` at the end of the run.
- **Task-audit drains:** how often a full task-audit batch drained the pool.

### Expected

The model behind the numbers: 0.3 s of overhead per `tp done` and spawn, 0.2 s per worktree add,
0.1 s per pick and per loop re-read, and the gate's 1 s. Wall times are that model's, rounded; the
other columns follow from the schedule, not from the constants.

| Cap | Schedule | Wall (s) | Idle slot-s | Peak gates | Gate reuse | Solo reopens | Task-audit drains |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | barrier binary | 20.4 | 0.6 | 1 | n/a | 0 | 0 |
| 1 | rolling | 20.4 | 0.6 | 1 | n/a | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | barrier | 19.8 | 19.8 | 2 | 3/6 | 0 | 0 |
| 2 | rolling | 16.7 | 13.6 | 2 | 2/6 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | barrier | 18.4 | 35.4 | 2 | 2/6 | 0 | 0 |
| 3 | rolling | 12.3 | 17.1 | 3 | 1/6 | 0 | 0 |

- **Cap 1** is the same path on both binaries, so its two rows must agree within noise. That is
  the fixture's own check.
- **Barrier.** Cap 2 runs three batches of two, `{t1,t2}`, `{t3,t4}`, `{t5,t6}`, each waiting for
  its 4 s unit; cap 3 runs two batches of three, each holding one 4 s unit. The first close of a
  batch reuses its gate and the rest do not, so cap 2 reuses 3 and cap 3 reuses 2.
- **Rolling.** A short unit's slot is refilled as soon as it is integrated, so the 4 s units no
  longer hold the short ones back. Reuse falls, because the pool keeps landing work on the main
  branch under a running unit: at cap 2 only `t2` and `t5` find nothing landed since their add, at
  cap 3 only `t2`.
- **Peak gates** is at most the cap under the serial rule: a main gate runs only after a unit has
  exited, and its slot is not refilled until the gate ends, so at most `cap − 1` unit gates run
  beside it. A measured peak above the cap would mean a refill ran during a main gate.
- **Task-audit drains are 0 by construction:** a full task-audit batch is 10 files
  (`TaskAuditBatchFiles`), and six one-file tasks never fill one. The drain is shown by a Go test
  instead, not by this fixture.
- **Solo reopens are 0** because no two tasks touch the same file.

### Actual

`REPS=3 ./scripts/measure-rolling-implement.sh /tmp/tp-dev/tp /tmp/tp-dev/tp-barrier <dir>`, on
one machine with nothing else heavy running. The rolling binary is this branch's; the barrier
binary is built from `3158f7d8`. Each cell is the mean of the three repetitions, with the range in
brackets where they differ.

| Cap | Schedule | Wall (s) | Idle slot-s | Peak gates | Gate reuse | Solo reopens | Task-audit drains |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | barrier binary | 21.1 [20.9–21.3] | 0.7 [0.6–0.7] | 1 | n/a | 0 | n/a |
| 1 | rolling | 21.6 [20.9–22.1] | 0.8 [0.6–0.9] | 1 | n/a | 0 | n/a |
| 2 | barrier | 20.1 [19.2–21.4] | 18.2 [16.9–20.6] | 2 | 3/6 | 0 | n/a |
| 2 | rolling | 14.3 [13.5–15.5] | 7.9 [6.6–9.8] | 2 | 3/6, 3/6, 4/6 | 0 | n/a |
| 3 | barrier | 17.7 [17.1–18.1] | 32.3 [30.5–33.3] | 2 | 2/6 | 0 | n/a |
| 3 | rolling | 12.5 [12.3–12.8] | 16.5 [15.5–17.7] | 2 | 1/6 | 0 | n/a |

Every run closed all six tasks and stopped on `cap-units`.

- **Cap 1 agrees.** The two binaries' ranges overlap, and an earlier single repetition had them
  the other way round (barrier 22.0 s, rolling 19.8 s). The fixture's own check holds.
- **Rolling beats the barrier at both caps.** Wall time falls by 29% at cap 2 and at cap 3, and
  idle slot-seconds fall by more than half at cap 2 and by about half at cap 3. Cap 3 lands on the
  expected wall time. Cap 2 runs 2.4 s faster than expected while the barrier's cap 2 is within
  0.3 s of its own; this run does not say which part of the model was off.
- **Gate reuse at rolling cap 2 is 3 or 4 of 6, not the expected 2.** The model was wrong here,
  not the code. It assumed a refilled unit always finds work landed under it. The cap-2 log shows
  the opposite: `t3` is added after `t2` lands, finishes while `t1` still runs, and nothing lands
  between its add and its pick, so it reuses. `t5` does the same beside `t4`. In the repetition
  with four, `t1` and `t3` landed close enough together that `t4` and `t5` started at once; then
  `t6`, added after `t5` landed, also finished before `t4`. In the first logged repetition `t4`
  landed while `t6` ran, so `t6` did not reuse. In both logs read, neither 4 s unit reused.
- **Peak gates at rolling cap 3 is 2, not the expected 3.** The serial rule allows three: two unit
  gates beside one main gate. It does not force three. With 1 s gates and these sleeps, no three
  gates overlapped in any repetition. No run went above its cap, which is the claim the column
  exists to check.
- **Solo reopens are 0,** as expected, for the reason above.
- **Task-audit drains are n/a, not 0.** The Expected section's reason is wrong: the fixture
  (`scripts/measure-rolling-implement.sh`) sets only `run_max_parallel` and `run_max_units` and no
  `task_audit_roles`, so no task audit is ever due, whatever the batch size. The column measures
  nothing in this fixture. The drain is covered by `TestTaskAuditUnits_ADueTaskAuditDrainsAPool`.

## Mutation run

`./scripts/mutation.sh v1.24.0` over the 20 engine files changed since v1.24.0. The first run
mutated them whole, as the script then did; it was stopped by hand at the seventh file after 1 h
21 min (`~/.cache/tp-mut/cb4f88d5-20261118-213226`, no score). Survivors are classified only on
lines the release changed, and a dry count (`go-mutesting --dry-run`, the script's mutator set)
puts 524 mutants on the 20 files whole and 77 on their changed lines. v1.24.0's run had the same
shape: 13 of its 158 escapes on changed lines (`spec/1.24.0-measurements.md`). So the script now
passes `--git-diff-lines` (commit `1c5a8768`, `docs/lessons.md`).

The line filter's `git diff` honours a global `diff.external`. With `difft` set there, the same dry
count gave **0 mutants**, which a real run would have reported as a pass. With a global config
carrying only the identity it gave 77. The script hands the run that config, runs in a `git clone`
of HEAD so the filter has history, and stops with exit 3 on a zero dry count.

The changed-lines run (`~/.cache/tp-mut/cb4f88d5-20261118-225951`): 77 mutants, 56 killed,
21 escaped, 0 not covered, 1,100 s wall. The 21 were read one by one:

- **Killed by a new test, 8,** each seen red with its mutant applied by hand in a copy and green
  on the code:
  - `driver.go:822` `lock != nil` negated: the driver kept its copy of the unit lock open past the
    spawn. `TestUnitLock_TheLockIsFreeOnceTheChildExits`.
  - `nextunits.go:85` `len(running) > 0` as `> 1`: one free slot beside one running unit got the
    main-tree brief. `TestNextUnits_OneFreeSlotBesideOneRunningUnitGetsThePoolBrief`.
  - `runstate.go:236` `== 0` negated and as `== 1`: leaving the pool dropped the other orphans'
    pids. `TestRunRecorder_LeavingThePoolKeepsTheOtherOrphans`.
  - `runstate.go:432` `PID > 0` as `>= 0` and `> -1`: `kill(0, 0)` succeeds, so a member with no
    recorded pid read as live. `TestLiveUnits_APoolMemberWithoutAPidIsNotLive`.
  - `runstate.go:439` the same two: a row without a pid hid the orphan's pid.
    `TestUnitPID_ARowWithoutAPidFallsBackToTheOrphansPid`.
- **Equivalent, 12:**
  - `boolOrder`'s 1 as 2 and 0 as -1 (the order is kept).
  - The run-lock poll's 10 ms as 9, 11 and `10 / time.Millisecond` (a busy loop, same result).
  - `implementbatch.go:38` `maxParallel <= 1` as `< 1` and `<= 0`: at cap 1 the pool path takes the
    lead alone or nothing beside a running unit, as `aloneOnceDrained` does.
  - `runstate.go:232` and `:236` `== 0` as `== -1`: an empty pool or orphan map is kept instead of
    nil, and `omitempty` writes both the same.
  - `runstate.go:432` and `:439` `> 0` as `> 1`: pid 1 is init and never a unit's child.
  - `runstate.go:385` `len(st.Pool) == 0` as `== -1`: a cycle with no pool then probes the run lock
